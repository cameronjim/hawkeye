## Act 1: Night Shift

Early December, one year after the Tracksuit Mafia mess. New York is lit up for Christmas, and Kate Bishop is out on patrol with a bow, a dog in a sweater, and a rent payment she's three weeks late on. The game opens mid-chase as Kate pursues a bike thief across the rooftops of the Lower East Side, narrating her own heroics to Lucky, who is not impressed. She botches a grapple arrow, lands in a dumpster full of wrapping paper, and still catches the guy. The press has taken to calling her "Hawkeye's sidekick," which she hates, and Clint keeps texting from the farm telling her to take a night off. She does not take a night off.

The early missions are pure fun. A department store's animated sleigh display gets stolen. A toy drive gets robbed by a crew in reindeer masks. A mall Santa turns out to be running a fencing operation out of his grotto. Kate unlocks her trick arrows through gags: a sticky foam arrow for traversal and cleanup, a boomerang arrow that returns at the worst possible moment, a confetti arrow she insists is tactical. Her civilian life runs alongside it. She's job hunting, dodging her landlord, and visiting her mother Eleanor in prison out of obligation, leaving every time more confused about what she feels. Clint cameos over video call from the Barton farm, with Laura and the kids heckling Kate in the background.

Through all of this, small things sit in the corners of the frame. A courier is found dead with a single arrow-sized wound. A target-shaped ornament shows up on Kate's fire escape. A string of petty crimes seems to be circling the same few addresses. Kate laughs it off, and the player may not.

Tomas, one of the Tracksuit Mafia survivors, has been watching Kate for weeks. He's been trying to rebuild the crew's old business in the shadow of the family's collapse, and he has a grudge, but he's also easygoing about it. When he finally confronts her, it's on a rooftop above a closed social club, with a handful of his remaining guys in matching tracksuits and a portable speaker playing Christmas music. He's almost apologetic. He tells Kate he has to do this, because he made a promise to the guys, and he'd feel bad if he didn't at least try. Kate says she gets it.

The boss fight is a formality, and the game knows it. Tomas's crew fights with oversized holiday props, and Tomas himself is a decent brawler who keeps stopping to compliment her form and complain about his knees. The fight teaches the combat loop of dodging, arrow mix-ups, and trick arrow finishers, all inside a snowy rooftop arena with string lights and a collapsing inflatable Santa. When Tomas goes down, he laughs, tells her it was a good try, and asks if she's still coming to the Christmas dinner at the club. She is. The NYPD takes him in, and he waves at Kate from the back of the cruiser.

Before the act ends, Tomas gives her a warning that lands as an afterthought. Someone has been asking about the old crew and the Sloan money, and he'd stay out of it if he were her. Kate shrugs it off, and the act ends on a quiet shot of the city lit up for the holidays.

## Act 2: Pieces

The tone shifts. Kate follows up on Tomas's warning and finds that the dead courier was not an isolated case. A lawyer, an accountant, and a driver have all died in the last few weeks, and every one of them was connected to Eleanor's old business through the Sloan money. Each was killed by a shot that should have been impossible, bending around corners, ricocheting off lamps, and threading through gaps in traffic. The NYPD points at Clint, because the style looks like his Ronin-era precision, and Kate heads upstate to find him.

Clint becomes playable here in a quiet, funny sequence at the farm that eases players into his controls. His play style is the opposite of Kate's. He's grounded and deliberate, with a focus mode that slows time for long-range shots and a hearing loss mechanic that muffles the audio whenever he takes damage. He doesn't traverse the city the way Kate does. He relies on sightlines, cover, and elevated positions, and the level design opens up differently for him. Switching between the two mid-mission becomes the core hook of the game.

Together they follow the killer's trail through Rockefeller Center, a shuttered circus warehouse in Queens, and an abandoned ice factory in Brooklyn. The technique is unmistakable to Clint. He finally admits that it's the work of his older brother Barney, who trained him in a traveling circus and left him behind when they were teenagers. Barney is now Trickshot, a contract assassin, and Clint has spent decades wondering whether he could have stopped him. The reveal is quiet and lets Kate see just how much her mentor has been carrying.

The midpoint set piece is the Central Park Christmas market and Wollman Rink. Trickshot has rigged the string lights with explosive arrows, and the market collapses onto the ice while you play both characters through panicking crowds. The first Trickshot boss fight is a rooftop duel where you swap between Kate and Clint to flank him. He escapes, leaving Clint a circus ticket from their childhood.

Tomas reappears in the back half of the act. Out on bail and unusually serious, he tells Kate he's heard the same name the killer has been using in the underworld. He hands over a lead on where the shipments of Sloan money were routed, and he asks Kate, quietly, to take care of herself. It's the first time he doesn't make a joke.

Maya Lopez shows up late in the act as a wary ally. She tells them Wilson Fisk survived and is rebuilding, and that he hired Barney to clean up the Sloan trail because Eleanor is the last person who knows where the money went. She's up for parole, and if she talks, Fisk loses everything. If she walks, she's an easy target.

## Act 3: Christmas Eve

Kate learns the whole picture. Her mother killed a man and lied about it for years, and now the people who want her dead are the people she once worked for. Kate has to decide how far she'll go to protect her. Clint doesn't tell her what to do. He just says he spent years wishing someone had stood between him and Barney.

Fisk stages a prison transfer ambush on Christmas Eve, using the chaos of a snowstorm and holiday traffic. The playable sequence alternates between Kate on the road with Eleanor's convoy and Clint on a rooftop trying to overwatch. Eleanor ends up in Kate's care, and the two have a raw conversation in the middle of the chaos that neither of them forgets.

The final boss is Trickshot in three phases on a snowed-in Brooklyn Bridge. Phase one is a long-range duel with both Hawkeyes. Phase two is close-quarters with blades and trick arrows as Barney closes the distance. Phase three is a collapsing finish where the bridge starts to fail and Kate and Clint have to land simultaneous shots. Kate refuses to kill him. Clint faces his brother and finally says what he's held in for thirty years. Barney is taken alive.

## Ending

Fisk escapes, but now Maya, Kate, and Clint all know where to look. Eleanor agrees to testify. On Christmas morning at the Barton farm, Kate gets a seat at the table and a gift from Clint: his old bow. Tomas shows up with a tin of cookies from the social club and sits next to Lucky, who finally takes an interest in someone.

In a post-credits scene, a visitor at Eleanor's cell window is someone other than Kate, asking a question that tells us the next villain is already moving.
